01 Segmentation
Enough of this 'Generation Z' bullshit
Every few weeks we see blogs and op-eds trying to explain behaviour through generational labels — as if everyone born within a 20-year window shares the same motivations. It's neat. It's easy. And it quietly kills real insight.
The patterns that matter don't sit in age bands. They sit in how people are actually living.
In our segmentation work the gaps show up quickly. People grouped into the same generation often behave completely differently once you understand their life stage, pressure and context. That's often where the opportunity is missed — and where better decisions start.

  Rural Pulse 02
Food security isn't a supply issue. It's a confidence one.

The food conversation in New Zealand is usually framed around production and export strength. Through our Rural Pulse programme we're seeing something closer to home. People aren't questioning whether New Zealand can produce enough food — they're questioning whether they'll be able to access it reliably.

Affordability, competing land use and future uncertainty are reshaping confidence in the system itself. That gap between national capability and personal confidence is where trust is won or lost.

03 Ethnography
IKEA didn't land in New Zealand. It was rewritten by it.

When IKEA opened at Sylvia Park the story wasn't a global giant arriving; it was a global model adapting. Nationwide pickup points, hybrid journeys, circularity from day one — all shaped by how New Zealanders actually live and shop.

Distance isn't abstract, convenience isn't negotiable, and effort plays a defining role in choice. That's the difference between rollout and relevance.

  Education 04
The problem with vocational education isn't what it offers. It's what it signals.
The shift in tertiary decision-making is often framed as a student problem. It isn't. Vocational pathways are credible, practical and increasingly aligned to how people want to build careers. The hesitation sits elsewhere.
It sits with the influencers.
Parents and careers advisers still hold a powerful, often unspoken role in shaping what “good” looks like — and vocational training is too often positioned as a fallback, not a first choice. That gap between a strong offer and a second-tier perception is where uptake falters.
